# Agriculture Note Learning

Things learned about farming in Kurdistan while building the wheat drought alarm.

## Sowing and rain

- (2026-10-08) "If rain is severely delayed into late December, desperate farmers sometimes bury seed in dry dirt anyway, praying winter storms will eventually come."

## Farmer choices in uncertain years

- (2026-10-08) "In years that look uncertain, farmers often spend less on expensive fertilizers or choose hardier barley over wheat, since barley tolerates drought much better."

## Decision points in the season

- (2026-10-08) **At the start (Nov/Dec):** "Farmers sow after the first deep rain (Pelê), taking a calculated risk with no way to guarantee how much rain will follow."
- (2026-10-08) **At the midpoint (Feb/March):** "Halfway through the 6-month cycle, knowing whether it is a drought year gives farmers a clear decision window to cut further expenses (fertilizers, weedkillers) and salvage the green crop for animal feed rather than suffering a total financial loss by harvest time."

## Satellites for irrigation

- (2026-10-08) "Satellites track daily water loss (**evapotranspiration**) from crops and soil by measuring plant canopy cover and sun exposure.
  Thermal and radar sensors detect unseen **crop stress and rising leaf temperature** the moment root-zone moisture runs critically low.
  Software converts this data into a clear schedule, alerting the farmer the exact day and hours to run their pumps."
  - Claude's check: true in principle, but thermal sensors see leaf temperature and radar sees soil moisture. Free satellites pass every few days and clouds block them, so it is not "the moment". The exact hours come from weather forecasts plus a soil-water model. This is for irrigated farms with pumps, not rain-fed wheat.
