# AI + Satellite Remote Sensing for Crop Monitoring, Yield Forecasting, Drought Early Warning and Food-Security Planning (real-world deployments)

Research date: 2026-10-07. Labels used: **[OPERATIONAL]** = runs routinely and its output is used by an institution; **[PILOT/RESEARCH]** = study or trial, no confirmed routine use; **[CLAIM]** = stated by the operator or vendor, not independently verified. A general caution: most long-running national/global systems (FEWS NET, GEOGLAM, ASAP, CGMS-Maroc, FASAL) combine satellite indices (NDVI, satellite rainfall) with agro-met models, statistics and analyst judgment. "AI" in the deep-learning sense shows up mainly in newer systems (NASA Harvest, India YES-TECH) and in research papers.

## 1. Which systems forecast yield/production or detect drought from satellites at national/regional scale, how accurate, how early, and did governments act?

### Takeaway
Several satellite-fed early-warning and forecasting systems have run for 10+ years (FEWS NET, GEOGLAM Crop Monitor, JRC ASAP, Morocco's CGMS-Maroc, India's FASAL). The best evidence of accuracy is for FEWS NET: independent checks find 78-84% of its projections correct, with accuracy lowest exactly where it matters most (severe food insecurity). Yield forecasts in dry cereal systems such as Morocco work about 2 months before harvest, and research models claim up to 4 months. Clear proof that a government acted on the output exists only for some cases: Ethiopia 2015/16, and Ukraine 2022, where the ministry confirmed the estimates.

### Cited Findings
**FEWS NET (USAID-funded; analyst-driven, uses satellite rainfall/vegetation inputs) [OPERATIONAL]**
- An independent evaluation (Bertetti, Agnolucci, Calzadilla, Capra, UCL, Oct 2024) found FEWS NET food-security projections had "an overall high accuracy of 78% across diverse timeframes and locations", with "significant variations in performance across distinct regions and prediction periods" — [arXiv 2410.09384](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.09384)
- A separate validation of FEWS NET projections in Africa for 2009-2020 found them accurate in ~84% of cases. Above 93% of projections were accurate at the lowest food-insecurity level, accuracy fell as severity rose, and there was a bias toward over-projecting severe food insecurity (based on the abstract/snippet; I could not open the full text) — [Global Food Security, 2021](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2211912421000201)
- In Ethiopia, FEWS NET was "generally accurate", but its deviations grew during climate events like El Niño and were largest in the most food-insecure regions — [Food Security (Springer), 2019](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12571-019-00909-y)
- Another study looked at how complex events reduce early-warning skill in the Greater Horn of Africa — [Global Food Security, 2020](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2211912420300274)
- FEWS NET reports are compiled and released every four months (projection cycle) — [Wikipedia: FEWS NET](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Famine_Early_Warning_Systems_Network); FEWS NET says it is working on longer-lead forecasts of weather extremes — [FEWS NET innovation white paper, 2025](https://fews.net/sites/default/files/2025-08/White%20Paper%20-%20Innovation.pdf)

**GEOGLAM Crop Monitor for AMIS / for Early Warning [OPERATIONAL]**
- The G20 set up GEOGLAM and AMIS in 2011 after the 2008 and 2010/11 food-price spikes. Since September 2013 the Crop Monitor for AMIS has published consensus crop-condition assessments for wheat, rice, maize and soy in the major producer and exporter countries, covering close to 80% of global production of those crops — [GEOGLAM Crop Monitor, About](https://www.cropmonitor.org/about-us); [Becker-Reshef et al., Global Food Security, "The GEOGLAM crop monitor for AMIS"](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S221191241830155X); [timeline](https://www.cropmonitor.org/our-history-timeline)
- A sister product, the Crop Monitor for Early Warning, covers countries at risk of food insecurity and has passed 100 issues — [NASA Harvest](https://www.nasaharvest.org/news/geoglam-crop-monitor-for-early-warning-releases-100th-issue); [ResearchGate paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/338432829_Strengthening_agricultural_decisions_in_countries_at_risk_of_food_insecurity_The_GEOGLAM_Crop_Monitor_for_Early_Warning)

**JRC ASAP (Anomaly hot Spots of Agricultural Production, European Commission) [OPERATIONAL]**
- Each month, ASAP publishes a hotspot assessment for 81 food-insecure countries. Its first step is automatic: every sub-national unit (GAUL level 1) gets a warning level from "none" to 4, based on satellite rainfall estimates and NDVI anomalies. Analysts then review these warnings. The system targets production deficits in water-limited agricultural systems worldwide, and warnings are also issued every ten days — [ASAP portal](https://agricultural-production-hotspots.ec.europa.eu/); [ASAP warning classification v4.0](https://agricultural-production-hotspots.ec.europa.eu/files/asap_warning_classification_v_4_0.pdf); [JRC Africa Knowledge Platform](https://africa-knowledge-platform.ec.europa.eu/node/31999)

**India: FASAL / MNCFC national crop forecasts [OPERATIONAL]**
- MNCFC regularly produces district-, state- and national-level forecasts for 9 major crops using Space Applications Centre (ISRO) methods. It uses both optical and microwave (radar) data for crop area, crop condition and production forecasts — [ISPRS Archives 2019](https://isprs-archives.copernicus.org/articles/XLII-3-W6/223/2019/)
- Area and production estimates improved as image resolution rose from 23.5 m (Resourcesat-2 LISS-III) to 10 m (Sentinel-2). The paper compared FASAL with official DES wheat estimates for 2013-2017. I did not retrieve the exact deviation percentages — [ISPRS Archives 2019 PDF](https://isprs-archives.copernicus.org/articles/XLII-3-W6/223/2019/isprs-archives-XLII-3-W6-223-2019.pdf)

**India: YES-TECH, satellite yield estimation for crop insurance (PMFBY) [OPERATIONAL, contested]**
- From Kharif 2023, the government uses satellite remote sensing plus models (YES-TECH) to estimate paddy and wheat yields, after MNCFC pilot studies. YES-TECH yield gets a mandatory 30% weight in the final yield used for claims. Madhya Pradesh gave it 100% weight over crop-cutting experiments for soybean from Kharif 2023 — [PIB, Govt of India, Dec 2024](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2085179); [Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/agriculture/can-technology-make-indias-crop-insurance-payouts-more-accurate)
- [CLAIM] The government says that in Kharif 2023 "all implementing states successfully completed claims calculation and payout using YES-TECH without any disputes from stakeholders" — [PIB, Dec 2024](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2085179). Down To Earth's reporting from Madhya Pradesh contradicts this (see Q4).

**Ukraine war-time estimates (NASA Harvest, 2022) [OPERATIONAL during the emergency; ministry-validated]**
- NASA Harvest made an in-season crop-type map of Ukraine at 3 m resolution with 94% accuracy, checked against about 4,700 ground samples. It estimated 94% of the winter crop was harvested, including 88% in areas outside Ukrainian control. It estimated 26.6 Mt of wheat harvested in 2022, "several million tons higher than expected in leading forecasts", with 5.8 Mt from areas outside Ukrainian control — [NASA Earth Observatory, 2022](https://earthobservatory.nasa.gov/images/150590/larger-wheat-harvest-in-ukraine-than-expected); [NASA Harvest](https://www.nasaharvest.org/news/farming-warzone-nasa-harvest-releases-satellite-based-ukraine-wheat-production-estimates)
- A second NASA Harvest release gave Ukraine wheat production as 25-26.5 Mt (21-22 Mt in Ukrainian-controlled areas, 4.1-4.4 Mt in Russian-occupied areas). It is not clear from my sources whether this is a different season or an earlier 2022 estimate, so treat the two releases as separate — [NASA Harvest](https://www.nasaharvest.org/news/farming-warzone-nasa-harvest-releases-satellite-based-ukraine-wheat-production-estimates)
- Ukraine's Ministry of Agrarian Policy confirmed the results: "The actual data we are gradually receiving directly from the fields in the territories controlled by Ukraine are very close to the estimates obtained in cooperation with NASA" (Denys Palamarchuk) — [Planet Pulse / NASA Harvest](https://www.planet.com/pulse/nasa-harvest-larger-wheat-harvest-in-ukraine-than-expected/)

**Morocco: CGMS-Maroc**. See Q5. In short: operational, forecasts at national, agro-ecological zone and province level, with useful accuracy from about 2 months before harvest — [INRA Morocco](https://www.inra.org.ma/en/content/cgms-maroc-drought-early-warning-and-cereal-yield-forecasting-system-morocco)

### Inferences
- FEWS NET's 78-84% accuracy belongs to the whole analyst-plus-satellite system, not to a satellite model on its own. Its weak spot is the high-severity end, where it tends to over-warn. For a drought tool this is arguably the safer error, but it costs credibility.
- The systems with a documented link to government action either faced an obvious, high-stakes shock (Ukraine 2022, Ethiopia 2015) or are written into a payout rule (India PMFBY, index insurance). Routine bulletins like Crop Monitor and ASAP are widely read, but I found no published study that traces a specific government decision back to them.

### Gaps
- I found no documented case where GEOGLAM or AMIS output directly triggered a government import or reserve decision. Their impact is described in general terms ("market transparency") only.
- I did not retrieve FASAL's numerical deviation from official estimates; the ISPRS paper has it.
- Unverified: FEWS NET's operations were disrupted in 2025 by US foreign-aid funding cuts. I did not confirm the details or current status in this session.
- I did not retrieve USDA FAS IPAD accuracy statistics, though the system is operational and covers Iraq and Kazakhstan (see [Crop Explorer Iraq 2022/23](https://ipad.fas.usda.gov/cropexplorer/chart.aspx?regionid=metu&ftypeid=47&fattributeid=1&fctypeid=24&fcattributeid=6&cntryid=IRQ&startdate=9%2F1%2F2022&enddate=6%2F30%2F2023&nationalGraph=False)).
- I could not open the JRC MARS (EU) in-season wheat-forecast evaluation ("In-season performance of European Union wheat forecasts during extreme impacts", [PMC6194012](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6194012/)) because of a CAPTCHA, so I have no MARS accuracy numbers.

## 2. Where has satellite-based early warning changed a decision with documented impact ($ benefit, aid, payouts, anticipatory action)?

### Takeaway
The documented money figures come from anticipatory action evaluations. In Mongolia, early action against the 2017/18 dzud returned $7.1 per $1, delivered about 4 months before a normal emergency response. Kenya's 2017 pre-drought animal-feed distribution returned $3.5 per $1. A cross-region FAO-WFP average is about $2.34 per $1. Satellite-indexed insurance (Kenya KLIP, African Risk Capacity) has paid real money to over 20,000 households a year. ARC's 2016 Malawi failure shows the model's crop settings matter as much as the satellite data.

### Cited Findings
**Anticipatory action return on investment [evaluated; FAO/WFP self-evaluations]**
- Mongolia 2017/18 dzud (harsh winter): every $1 invested in early action gave herders $7.1 in avoided losses and added benefits. The action came 4 months before the standard emergency response — [PreventionWeb / FAO "Mongolia: Impact of early warning early action"](https://www.preventionweb.net/publications/view/61581); [FAO KORE](https://www.fao.org/in-action/kore/publications/publications-details/fr/c/1295686/)
- Kenya 2017 drought: animal feed distributed before the drought peaked had a cost-benefit ratio of 3.5 ($3.5 benefit per $1) — [FAO, Impact of Disasters on Agriculture and Food 2023, §4.2](https://openknowledge.fao.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/19764bec-3b8e-46a6-8680-1dbab43bccaa/content/impact-of-disasters-on-agriculture-and-food-2023/anticipatory-action-interventions.html)
- FAO's figure of "up to $7 per $1" comes from a ten-country study. A recent cross-region FAO-WFP evaluation gives an average return of $2.34 per $1. FAO has run more than 50 anticipatory action interventions (drought, cold wave, pests, animal disease, COVID-19) — [FAO 2023 §4.2](https://www.fao.org/3/cc7900en/online/impact-of-disasters-on-agriculture-and-food-2023/anticipatory-action-interventions.html); [FAO-WFP AA Strategy, Sept 2023](https://reliefweb.int/report/world/fao-wfp-anticipatory-action-strategy-scaling-anticipatory-actions-prevent-food-crises-september-2023)
- WFP's Nepal (flood) return-on-investment study: every $1 spent before a disaster saves up to $34 compared with responding afterwards. This is a flood case, not drought — [WFP Anticipatory Action](https://www.wfp.org/anticipatory-actions)
- Current scale: the FAO-WFP Joint Anticipatory Action Appeal for El Niño (June 2026-March 2027) seeks $202 million to protect up to 8.8 million people in 22 high-risk countries — [FAO, 2026](https://openknowledge.fao.org/handle/20.500.14283/ce0220en)
- WFP has published M&E guidance for drought anticipatory actions under Forecast-based Financing — [WFP](https://www.wfp.org/publications/monitoring-and-evaluation-anticipatory-actions-drought-guidance-and-tools-forecast)

**Ethiopia 2015/16 El Niño drought: early warning plus government action [documented]**
- Early-warning systems alerted the government after the 2015 spring and summer rains failed. UN OCHA issued a clear warning in August 2015. The government committed about $300 million of its own money, and Ethiopia was not listed as at risk of famine. The credit is given to the government's response, a major contrast with 1973 and 1984 — [World Peace Foundation](https://worldpeacefoundation.org/blog/ethiopia-no-longer-the-land-of-famine/)
- At the April 2016 peak, 10.2 million people were targeted with food assistance and another 7.9 million were covered by the Productive Safety Net Programme. The estimated $1.4 billion requirement was less than half funded — [ALNAP: Ethiopia lessons learned from the El Niño drought 2015-16](https://library.alnap.org/system/files/content/resource/files/main/ETHIOPIA%20LESSONS%20LEARNED%20FROM%20THE%20EL-%20NI%C3%91O%20DROUGHT%202015-16.pdf)

**Kenya Livestock Insurance Program (KLIP): satellite NDVI-indexed, government-paid premiums [OPERATIONAL]**
- Payouts trigger automatically when satellite (NDVI) forage scarcity falls below a set threshold for an insured area, and every registered pastoralist in that area is paid — [Business Daily](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/markets/sh214-million-livestock-insurance-payout-for-drought-hit-pastoralists--2140236)
- In 2017 KLIP paid KSh 494 million to about 22,000 vulnerable pastoralist households. Over its first five years, more than US$10 million was paid out — [CGSpace (ILRI)](https://cgspace.cgiar.org/items/43a80bc8-cb33-4979-989b-2668d63f3e68); [World Bank / Financial Protection Forum](https://www.financialprotectionforum.org/sites/default/files/CountryProfile%20Kenya%20Brochure%20HighRes.pdf); [UC Davis BASIS](https://basis.ucdavis.edu/news/government-kenya-partners-insurers-make-record-insurance-payouts-livestock-herders-facing)
- Payouts ranged from KSh 1,450 to KSh 29,400 per household depending on the satellite-measured severity — [Business Daily](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/markets/sh214-million-livestock-insurance-payout-for-drought-hit-pastoralists--2140236)
- In a World Bank beneficiary survey, most recipients said payouts were big enough to help maintain livestock, but many reported that claim payments arrived late — [World Bank KLIP perception report](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099653111072339695/pdf/IDU0f42240a300dfc046280890c0efdd68ff1ec0.pdf)

**African Risk Capacity (ARC): sovereign drought insurance driven by the Africa RiskView satellite rainfall model [OPERATIONAL; documented failure]**
- Malawi declared a disaster in April 2016, with more than 6 million people facing food shortage, but ARC's model initially showed no payout. The cause was that the model assumed long-cycle maize while many farmers had switched to short-cycle varieties. The satellite rainfall itself matched ground rainfall data. After Malawi disputed the result, ARC recalibrated the model and paid US$8 million (press release dated 14 Nov 2016, about 7 months after the disaster declaration) — [Artemis](https://www.artemis.bm/news/african-risk-capacity-in-8m-parametric-insurance-payout-to-malawi/); [ARC press release, Nov 2016](https://www.africanriskcapacity.org/2016/11/14/press-release-malawi-to-receive-usd-8m-insurance-payout-to-support-drought-affected-families/)
- Later ARC payouts to Malawi totalled $11.6 million (date not confirmed from snippet; probably the 2024 El Niño drought) — [The Insurer](https://www.theinsurer.com/ti/news/malawi-drought-triggers-11-6mn-african-risk-capacity-payouts/). Bloomberg (2024) reported on the risks of insurance spreading across Africa as the climate warms — [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/features/2024-malawi-crop-insurance-climate-change/)
- An ARC document on its 2015 payouts reports reaching "at least 1.3 million" people (wording taken from the document title; I did not open the body) — [UNFCCC-hosted ARC document](https://unfccc.int/files/cooperation_and_support/financial_mechanism/standing_committee/application/pdf/payouts_and_successes.pdf)

### Inferences
- The strongest $-per-$ numbers (7.1, 3.5, 2.34) measure the value of acting early. They are not tests of the satellite model's accuracy. The satellite or forecast trigger enables the early action, but the evaluations do not separate out its contribution.
- Index-based payouts do reach people at scale, but the binding constraints are model calibration (ARC Malawi) and payment logistics (late KLIP payments). Satellite accuracy is not the main bottleneck.
- Even an evaluation-backed ROI does not guarantee funding: Ethiopia's 2015/16 response was less than half funded.

### Gaps
- The search snippets do not say which satellite index or trigger each FAO/WFP intervention used (seasonal forecast vs NDVI/VHI vs rainfall), so the ROI cannot be tied to "satellite AI" specifically.
- I found no documented case of a government changing food imports or reserve purchases because of a satellite yield forecast in the Middle East.
- I found no independent (non-FAO/WFP) audit of the 2.34 / 3.5 / 7.1 ratios. These are agency evaluations.
- Start Network and IFRC drought cost-benefit numbers were not retrieved.

## 3. Field-level crop type mapping, sown-area and crop-failure detection in operation: who does it and how accurately?

### Takeaway
Satellite mapping of every field is routine where a mandate requires it and good parcel data already exists: EU CAP area monitoring on Sentinel-1/2, India's FASAL acreage, and the NASA Harvest Ukraine map (94% at 3 m). In smallholder Africa, results of roughly 80-84% are mostly research-level, held back by clouds, small fields and intercropping.

### Cited Findings
- **EU CAP Area Monitoring System [OPERATIONAL, regulatory]**: the "regular and systematic observation, tracking and assessment of agricultural activities" on agricultural parcels using Copernicus Sentinel data (or equivalent). Sentinel-1 radar and Sentinel-2 optical images arrive every 3-5 days, and the system monitors parcels against the LPIS/GSAA registers used for CAP area-based payments — [Gov.ie, Area Monitoring System](https://www.gov.ie/ga/foilsiuchan/5ce8b-area-monitoring-system-ams/); [JRC Checks-by-Monitoring docs](https://jrc-cbm.readthedocs.io/en/latest/dias4cbm_intro.html). One example is Slovenia's national-scale CAP monitoring on Copernicus data — [Copernicus Data Space](https://dataspace.copernicus.eu/node/791)
- **NASA Harvest Ukraine [OPERATIONAL in 2022]**: in-season crop-type map at 3 m with 94% accuracy (about 4,700 ground samples) — [NASA Earth Observatory](https://earthobservatory.nasa.gov/images/150590/larger-wheat-harvest-in-ukraine-than-expected); [NASA Harvest](https://www.nasaharvest.org/news/farming-warzone-nasa-harvest-releases-satellite-based-ukraine-wheat-production-estimates)
- **India FASAL [OPERATIONAL]**: district-level crop acreage from optical and microwave data, now using 10 m Sentinel-2 — [ISPRS 2019](https://isprs-archives.copernicus.org/articles/XLII-3-W6/223/2019/). PMFBY also uses remote sensing to plan crop-cutting experiments, assess losses, settle yield disputes and assess "prevented sowing" — [RIS Dakshin newsletter](https://ris.org.in/newsletter/dakshin/feb/news2.pdf); [PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2085179)
- **Kazakhstan [OPERATIONAL, older evidence ~2011]**: satellite monitoring covers about 10 million ha of Northern Kazakhstan cropland. Every year it gives the Ministry of Agriculture spring soil moisture, sown areas, planting dates, crop rotation, in-season crop condition and productivity, using MODIS daily data plus IRS LISS/AWiFS with supervised classification — [ISPRS Archives XXXVIII-8-W20, 2011](https://isprs-archives.copernicus.org/articles/XXXVIII-8-W20/61/2011/)
- **Syria 2018 [PILOT → used for national estimate]**: GORS and NAPC, with FAO support, mapped 662,295 ha of wheat and 1,036,906 ha of barley from high-resolution imagery. They predicted about 1.3 Mt of wheat (later revised down after late-rain damage) and about 400,000 t of barley, "several weeks ahead of harvest". The article published no accuracy or validation metrics. It was the first time the technology was used in Syria — [FAO Near East, 27 Sep 2018](https://www.fao.org/neareast/news/details/Modern-technologies-boost-national-capacity-to-update-Syria-s-figures-on-cereal-production/en)
- **Smallholder Africa [PILOT/RESEARCH]**: Sentinel-2 crop-type mapping in Nigeria reached 84% overall accuracy — [Remote Sensing 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs13173523). In Burkina Faso, crop type and rice yield reached above ~80% accuracy in the rainy season with multi-date Sentinel-2 and Random Forest, but the dry season needs more work — [ScienceDirect RSASE 2022](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2352938522001288). Northern Malawi mapping combined Sentinel-1, Sentinel-2 and PlanetScope — [Remote Sensing 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs13040700)

### Inferences
- Operational field-level mapping grows from regulatory or payment needs (EU subsidies, India insurance), where parcel boundaries and ground data already exist. Without parcel registers, accuracy and adoption fall.
- Radar (Sentinel-1) is added in operational systems (EU AMS, FASAL) to deal with clouds. For a dry winter-cereal region, clouds matter less than in tropical Africa, but winter-season clouds can still create gaps.

### Gaps
- I found no published accuracy statistics for EU AMS at member-state level, or for Kazakhstan's ministry service.
- I found no operational system with published accuracy that separates "sown but failed" from "never sown". PMFBY's "prevented sowing" assessment uses remote sensing, but I found no accuracy numbers for it.
- I found no published accuracy for Digital Earth Africa or WorldCereal cropland and crop-type layers in this session.

## 4. Known limits and documented failures

### Takeaway
The recurring failure modes are well documented:
- forecasts underestimate extreme droughts, because extreme years fall outside the training data;
- model assumptions go stale (ARC Malawi, where farmers had switched maize varieties);
- clouds, small fields, intercropping and mixed pixels;
- forecasts are least accurate at the most severe food-insecurity levels;
- farmers distrust opaque satellite payouts (India).

### Cited Findings
- **Skill drops in extreme years (ML/DL)**: in a 2026 study of the 2012 US Corn Belt drought, county-level corn yield models suffered from distribution mismatch, because the extreme year's conditions fell outside historical training ranges. Sample weighting and feature selection helped classical ML but gave "little to no improvement" for the deep-learning model (VITA) — [Gupta, Ming, Mohler, arXiv 2608.17971, Aug 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17971)
- **Process models systematically underestimate drought and heat losses**: "most models detect but underestimate the impact of droughts and heatwaves on yield", which is a systematic underestimation of the yield response, not only a misrepresentation of exposure. This study covers global gridded crop models, not satellite models — [Environmental Research Letters 2022](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1748-9326/ac592e)
- **Optical sensors see the canopy top**: drought symptoms can appear on lower leaves first, so early-season optical data may underestimate drought impact. Optical indices track green biomass rather than grain yield directly. This is from a search-result summary; the attribution to a specific paper is uncertain, possibly the field-level corn forecasting paper — [Remote Sensing 2024](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs16091573); see also [Modanesi et al., WRR 2020](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2019WR025855) on soil-moisture information in drought
- **Clouds, small fields, intercropping**: persistent cloud cover in the growing season, varied field size and shape, and frequent cereal-legume intercropping make mapping in sub-Saharan Africa hard. Mixed pixels remain a problem even at Sentinel-2's 10-20 m — [Frontiers in Environmental Science 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fenvs.2020.00085/full); [Burkina Faso study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2352938522001288)
- **Stale crop parameters (ARC Malawi 2016)**: the model assumed long-cycle maize after farmers had switched to short-cycle varieties. The result was no payout for a drought affecting more than 6 million people until recalibration, and the US$8 M was paid in Nov 2016 — [Artemis](https://www.artemis.bm/news/african-risk-capacity-in-8m-parametric-insurance-payout-to-malawi/)
- **Accuracy falls at the severe end**: FEWS NET is most accurate at minimal food insecurity (above 93%) and less accurate at higher phases, with a bias toward over-projecting severe food insecurity — [Global Food Security 2021](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2211912421000201). Errors were larger during El Niño and in the most food-insecure areas of Ethiopia — [Food Security 2019](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12571-019-00909-y)
- **Adoption and trust (India YES-TECH)**: Madhya Pradesh farmers say "the satellites they use cannot see what eyes can". Kharif insurance payouts in Bhopal, Sehore and Vidisha fell from ₹217.91 crore to ₹55.2 crore over 2018-2024. One Sehore farmer lost over a third of a 2-ha soybean crop in Kharif 2025 and had received nothing a year later. Farmer unions protested in 2024-25. Note that the payout decline has several causes and is not proven to come from YES-TECH — [Down To Earth, "Beyond the Lens"](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/agriculture/beyond-the-lens)
- **Late payouts**: KLIP beneficiaries report claim payments arriving late — [World Bank](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099653111072339695/pdf/IDU0f42240a300dfc046280890c0efdd68ff1ec0.pdf)
- **Conflict breaks simple assumptions**: 250 m satellite time series (2000-2015) over areas seized by the Islamic State in Syria and Iraq showed several trajectories at once (cropland expansion, abandonment, and less high-intensity cropping), not the uniform abandonment commonly assumed — [Eklund et al., Lund University](https://portal.research.lu.se/en/publications/how-conflict-affects-land-use-agricultural-activity-in-areas-seiz/); [Environmental Peacebuilding library](https://www.environmentalpeacebuilding.org/library/show/LibraryItem-3178)
- **Models don't transfer well across years in dry continental wheat**: a 2026 Kazakhstan district-level wheat yield study explicitly reports "temporal transferability limits", with climate dominating over vegetation indices. Rainfall there is 250-450 mm a year, varies by over 35% between years, and yields routinely swing by more than 50% between seasons — [Agronomy 2026, 16(13):1264](https://www.citedrive.com/en/discovery/crop-masked-vegetation-indices-and-terraclimate-for-district-level-wheat-yield-prediction-in-kazakhstan-savi-advantage-climate-dominance-and-temporal-transferability-limits/); [PDF](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/agronomy/agronomy-16-01264/article_deploy/agronomy-16-01264.pdf)

### Inferences
- In drought years, harvested area can shrink well below sown area. In Syria, the 2021 wheat harvested area was about half of 2019 (see Q5). A model that reads green-up as "crop present" will overestimate production unless it detects failure late in the season. This is the "sown but failed" problem in practice.
- Every documented failure here is about the model, its calibration or its governance, not the satellite data. ARC's satellite rainfall matched the ground, but the crop assumption was wrong.

### Gaps
- I found no source quantifying irrigated vs rainfed misclassification error in operational systems.
- I found no source quantifying accuracy for separating "sown but failed" from "never sown".
- A search summary said that "June forecasts (~1-month lead) underestimated drought impacts by 10.4-78.4% in extreme years". I could not confirm which paper this comes from (possibly the JRC/EU wheat forecasts paper [PMC6194012](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6194012/), which was blocked by a CAPTCHA). Do not cite it without checking.

## 5. Dry, rainfed wheat/barley regions (Middle East, North Africa, Central Asia): operational systems and results

### Takeaway
**Morocco's CGMS-Maroc** is the clearest operational national system in a dry rainfed cereal region. It forecasts provincial yields about 2 months before harvest, and NDVI explains much of the wheat-yield variation in the main producing provinces. **Syria (FAO/GORS 2018) and Kazakhstan** have used satellite mapping for national crop estimates. **In Iraq**, the only satellite yield-modelling work I found, at Sulaimani with the KRG ministry, is research-level, and FAO/GIEWS publishes national cereal forecasts. I found no operational evidence for **Turkey, Iran or Jordan**.

### Cited Findings
**Morocco [OPERATIONAL]**
- INRA runs CGMS-Maroc together with DMN (the meteorological service) and DSS (the agricultural statistics service), with a web viewer at cgms-maroc.ma. It uses weather data on a 10x10 km grid, satellite imagery and ground observations, and forecasts cereal yield at national, agro-ecological zone and province level — [INRA Morocco](https://www.inra.org.ma/en/content/cgms-maroc-drought-early-warning-and-cereal-yield-forecasting-system-morocco); [INRA brochure PDF](https://www.inra.org.ma/sites/default/files/CGMS%20English%20Version_0.pdf)
- Forecasts were "relatively accurate and early, from two months before harvests". NDVI explains the largest share of wheat yield variation (partial R² above 40%) in 14 of 23 provinces, and those provinces produce more than 69.4% of national output — [ResearchGate: CGMS-MAROC paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/380184363_CGMS-MAROC_THE_DROUGHT_EARLY_WARNING_AND_CEREAL_YIELD_FORECASTING_SYSTEM_OF_MOROCCO); [Agrometeorological Cereal Yield Forecasting in Morocco](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/258807169_Agrometeorological_Cereal_Yield_Forecasting_in_Morocco)
- [RESEARCH] Machine-learning models combining satellite drought indices, weather data and climate indices predicted Moroccan cereal yield in January, 4 months before harvest, with R² = 0.88 and RMSE about 0.22 t/ha (Bouras et al. 2021) — [Remote Sensing 13(16):3101](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/13/16/3101/xml)
- [RESEARCH] Lehmann et al. (2020, GRL) studied early forecasting of Moroccan wheat yields from climatic drivers — [GRL](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2020GL087516). Another study compared empirical and mechanistic field-level wheat models in Moroccan rainfed areas — [in silico Plants 2024](https://academic.oup.com/insilicoplants/article/6/1/diad020/7390631)

**Syria [PILOT used for national estimate; FAO CFSAM operational]**
- 2018: GORS and NAPC, with FAO support, mapped 662,295 ha of wheat and 1,036,906 ha of barley from satellite imagery. They predicted about 1.3 Mt of wheat (revised down after late-rain damage) and about 400 kt of barley, several weeks before harvest. No accuracy metrics were published. FAO said the data could guide "public procurement and levels of price support" — [FAO Near East, Sep 2018](https://www.fao.org/neareast/news/details/Modern-technologies-boost-national-capacity-to-update-Syria-s-figures-on-cereal-production/en)
- 2021 (FAO crop and food supply assessment mission, CFSAM): poor rain, heatwaves, costly inputs and fuel, and limited irrigation shrank the harvestable area. Harvested wheat area was 787,000 ha, slightly over half of 2019. Wheat production was about 1.05 Mt, against 2.8 Mt in 2020 and a pre-crisis average of 4.1 Mt (2002-2011) — [FAO CFSAM via ReliefWeb, Dec 2021](https://reliefweb.int/report/syrian-arab-republic/special-report-2021-fao-crop-and-food-supply-assessment-mission-syrian)
- 2025: Enab Baladi headline: "Production accounts for 19% of demand: Wheat threatens hunger in Syria" (headline only) — [Enab Baladi, May 2025](https://english.enabbaladi.net/archives/2025/05/production-accounts-for-19-of-demand-wheat-threatens-hunger-in-syria/)
- FAO has published a historical assessment of agricultural drought impact in the region — [FAO OpenKnowledge](https://openknowledge.fao.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/ca073b16-a69e-4dfa-9f00-b9ddf539c226/content)

**Iraq, including the Kurdistan Region [national forecasts operational via FAO GIEWS / USDA FAS; local yield modelling is research]**
- FAO GIEWS forecast Iraq's 2021 cereal production at a slightly above-average 5.4 Mt, despite lower wheat and barley output in drought-hit areas — [FAO GIEWS Iraq country brief archive](https://www.fao.org/giews/countrybrief/country/IRQ/pdf_archive/IRQ_Archive.pdf)
- USDA FAS Crop Explorer publishes Iraq-specific satellite and agro-met charts by season (e.g., Sep 2022-Jun 2023) — [USDA FAS IPAD](https://ipad.fas.usda.gov/cropexplorer/chart.aspx?regionid=metu&ftypeid=47&fattributeid=1&fctypeid=24&fcattributeid=6&cntryid=IRQ&startdate=9%2F1%2F2022&enddate=6%2F30%2F2023&nationalGraph=False)
- [RESEARCH, directly relevant to Slemani] WorldPop (University of Southampton), the University of Sulaimani and the KRG Ministry of Agriculture and Water Resources modelled farm-level wheat yield in Sulaimani governorate at 10 m. They used Sentinel-2 greenness and productivity measures plus topographic and climatic covariates in a Bayesian multiple linear regression, designed for "low data settings of arid and semi-arid regions" and for places surveyors cannot reach for security reasons. The work used 2020 harvest data and was published in Science of the Total Environment vol. 869 (2023). The project page describes it as exploratory, with no confirmed ministry deployment. I did not retrieve accuracy metrics — [University of Southampton eprints](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/474993); [Southampton project page](https://southampton.ac.uk/research/projects/exploring-satellite-datasets-advanced-statistical-models-to-estimate-crop); [WorldPop blog](https://www.worldpop.org/blog/exploring-satellite-datasets-and-advanced-statistical-models-to-estimate-crop-production-in-iraq/)
- [RESEARCH] Satellite time series at 250 m (2000-2015) of areas in Syria and Iraq seized by the Islamic State showed mixed cropland trajectories (expansion, abandonment, less intensive cropping) — [Eklund et al., Lund University](https://portal.research.lu.se/en/publications/how-conflict-affects-land-use-agricultural-activity-in-areas-seiz/). Another study used GIS and remote sensing to analyse agriculture as a funding source for ISIS — [Food Policy, ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0306919216303219)
- [Reported, weak source] Media reports cited by CEOBS describe about a 20% decrease in non-cropland vegetation in the Kurdistan Region of Iraq after 2014, linked to the war — [CEOBS](https://ceobs.org/?p=2737)

**Kazakhstan / Central Asia [OPERATIONAL, older evidence]**
- Satellite monitoring of about 10 Mha of northern cropland is prepared every year for the Ministry of Agriculture (sown area, planting dates, crop condition, productivity) — [ISPRS 2011](https://isprs-archives.copernicus.org/articles/XXXVIII-8-W20/61/2011/)
- Kazakhstan is the world's 7th-largest wheat exporter, with 12+ Mha of harvested wheat. Rainfall is 250-450 mm a year, varies by more than 35% between years, and yields swing by more than 50% between seasons. In a 2026 study, district-level vegetation-index models (SAVI performed best) showed limits when transferred to other years — [Agronomy 2026](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/agronomy/agronomy-16-01264/article_deploy/agronomy-16-01264.pdf)
- USDA FAS IPAD publishes satellite-based crop highlights for Kazakhstan (e.g., Aug 2015) — [USDA FAS](https://ipad.fas.usda.gov/highlights/2015/08/kz_28aug2015/index.htm)

**Region-wide coverage**: JRC ASAP's automatic NDVI and rainfall warnings are designed for water-limited agricultural systems worldwide and run monthly for 81 food-insecure countries — [ASAP](https://agricultural-production-hotspots.ec.europa.eu/)

### Inferences
- In rainfed Mediterranean and semi-arid cereals, spring vegetation indices explain much of the yield variation (Morocco: NDVI partial R² above 40% in the main provinces). This makes a satellite yield signal stronger here than in cloudy, intercropped smallholder Africa. The downside is huge year-to-year swings, which push forecasts into the "extreme year" problem described in Q4.
- For Iraq and the Kurdistan Region, there is no published operational satellite yield system with accuracy statistics. The nearest is the Sulaimani research collaboration with the KRG ministry. That is both a gap and an opportunity.
- Syria 2021 shows why harvested area, not sown area, is the variable that matters in drought years: the harvested wheat area was about half of 2019.

### Gaps
- I found no operational satellite crop-forecasting system with results for Turkey, Iran or Jordan. TARSIM, TÜİK, Iran's agriculture ministry and Jordan's NCARE were not covered by my searches, so this is not evidence that none exists.
- I did not retrieve the accuracy metrics (R², RMSE) of the Sulaimani Sentinel-2 wheat model; the publisher pages were blocked. Check the paper (Sci. Total Environ. 869, 2023) directly.
- I did not find Iraq-specific WFP or iMMAP satellite crop-monitoring products in this session.
- I did not retrieve how Morocco's government uses CGMS-Maroc forecasts (e.g., timing of wheat import subsidies or tariff suspensions), though such a link is plausible.
