# Aim your satellite AI at Kurdistan's drought

AI helps farming most when it warns early about a bad season and someone acts on that warning. This use has the hardest numbers in the world. In FAO and WFP evaluations, acting early on warnings of drought and harsh winters returned **$2.34 to $7.1 for every $1** spent. In 2025 India sent an AI rain forecast to **38.8 million farmers**, and 31–52% of them changed their planting. Satellites now also decide real money. The EU checks every claimed farm field from space and corrected over **3 million hectares** of claims in 2024. The failures are just as clear. A plant-disease app scored 99% in the lab but only 31% on new photos. A satellite drought model in Malawi counted **20,594** affected people when **6.5 million** were hungry. In the Kurdistan Region, the biggest and best-known farm problem is the drought and water crisis. In 2025 about **2 million dunams** of rain-fed land failed, about **800,000 t** of wheat was lost (roughly **$520 million**), and Dukan fell to **24%** full. Our top pick for the hackathon is one **drought-and-water alarm** for the whole region. It says, months ahead, how dry the year will be and which zones and dams it will hit. The runner-ups are **counting Kurdistan's wheat from space** for the Baghdad quota fight, and a **dam-only water watch**. The honest catch: the top pick runs on the engine the team already built. The research says the content was right and the story was wrong. It must be one famous problem with one alarm, not four jobs.

Two things stand out. First, the biggest money gains come from early warning tied to action, not from better farming on single fields. Second, most long-running warning systems mix satellite data with weather models and human experts. "Deep-learning AI" is newer and shows up mostly in research. A pitch can honestly say "AI on satellite data". It should not say that AI alone has a proven track record.

## Failures come from bad models and no follow-up, not from bad satellites

In almost every documented failure, the satellite data was fine. In Malawi, satellite rain matched the ground gauges. The model failed because it assumed long-season maize after farmers had switched to short-season seed ([Artemis](https://www.artemis.bm/news/african-risk-capacity-in-8m-parametric-insurance-payout-to-malawi/)). The second pattern is that models are weakest in exactly the years that matter. Crop models trained on normal years under-call extreme droughts ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17971); [ERL](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1748-9326/ac592e)). The team's own data shows the same thing. The Dukan summer forecast called 2025 "below normal" but predicted 168 km² of lake when the real lake was 113 km² ([team backtest](../evidence/past_seasons/BACKTEST_RESULTS.md)).

The third pattern is that a warning nobody acts on saves nothing. In 2024, wheat rust spread for about a month through Raparin, where about 180,000 dunams of wheat are grown. The report mentions no forecast and no warning ([Rudaw](https://rudaw.net/english/kurdistan/04042024)). Ethiopia's rust system worked partly because officials used it to decide where to send their limited fungicide ([Allen-Sader et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7680955)). The fourth pattern is that satellite-only decisions lose trust. The successful money schemes mix satellite numbers with field checks: India's 30% weight, Turkey's village reference plots ([TARSIM](https://www.tarsim.gov.tr/staticweb/krm-web/mevzuatlar/genel-sartlar/2024/kbkvs-genel-sartlar.pdf)). The schemes that pay automatically also have the government paying the premiums. The last pattern is that venture-funded farmer services like Wefarm fade, while services run with the government over voice and SMS last (Ama Krushi, Ethiopia's 8028 hotline, India's M-Kisan SMS).

For the team, this means three things. Show the misses openly. Name the person who acts on each warning. Say "builds on FAO data", never "the first".

## Kurdistan's four biggest farm problems are one water story

| # | Problem | How big | How famous | Can satellite AI help? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Drought kills rain-fed wheat and barley | 2025: about **2 million dunams** of rain-fed land failed ([Rudaw](https://www.rudaw.net/english/kurdistan/090620251)). About **800,000 t** of KRI wheat lost ([Peregraf](https://peregraf.com/en/news/9102)), about **$520M** at the 2025 price (our calculation) | Very high | **High**: risk per zone, months ahead |
| 2 | Baghdad's wheat quota and late payments | Baghdad bought **700,000 t (2024) → 400,000 t (2025)** ([Rudaw](https://www.rudaw.net/english/kurdistan/250420261)). In 2026 only **292,000 t** at full price ([Kurdistan24](https://www.kurdistan24.net/en/story/919617/kurdistan-region-upgrades-wheat-procurement-strategy-as-record-harvest-challenges-local-supply-chains)). By Oct 2025 only **72.5B of 300B+ IQD** had been paid ([Rudaw](https://www.rudaw.net/english/kurdistan/14102025)) | Very high, every year | **High as neutral evidence**. It cannot force payment |
| 3 | Low dams and Iran's upstream dams | Dukan at **24%** in June 2025, a 20-year low. The lake shrank 56%, and **4 million** people live downstream ([AFP](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2605253/middle-east)). Dukan's power plant stopped ([Rudaw](https://www.rudaw.net/english/kurdistan/030520252)). The Little Zab fell **75%** in Raparin ([Rudaw](https://rudaw.net/english/kurdistan/070420252)) | Very high (AFP story seen worldwide) | **Medium-high**: measuring works, forecasting only partly |
| 4 | Groundwater over-pumping | **28,410** illegal wells. **1,650+** springs dried up ([Peregraf](https://peregraf.com/en/investigation/9492)) | Medium | **Medium**: sees irrigated fields, not wells |
| 5 | Prices, smuggling, Baghdad's bans | Halabja tomatoes at **100 IQD/kg** ([Rudaw](https://www.rudaw.net/english/kurdistan/25072025)). Baghdad banned **32** Kurdish products in Jan 2026 ([Kurdistan24](https://www.kurdistan24.net/ckb/story/890471)) | High | **Low**: a policy problem |
| 6 | Crop fires | **2,513 dunams** burned in Erbil, Jan–Jun 2024 ([Rudaw](https://rudaw.net/english/kurdistan/230620242)) | Medium | Easy to map, but about **1,000×** smaller than drought |
| 7 | Pesticide misuse | **28.5%** of 200 farmers (Duhok, Nineveh, Basra) admit using banned or restricted products ([KirkukNow](https://kirkuknow.com/en/news/71519)) | Low, rising | **Low**: satellites cannot see it |

Problems 1 to 4 are one chain. Little rain lowers the dams, and Iran's dams make it worse. Farmers then pump more groundwater, and the wheat fails anyway. Problems 1 and 2 also swap places with the weather. In a dry year like 2025, there is no crop. In a wet year there is no buyer. In 2026, 22 of 25 dams overflowed ([Rudaw](https://www.rudaw.net/english/kurdistan/250420261)), and the KRG expected more than 2.5 Mt of wheat. It asked Baghdad to buy 1.25 Mt ([Kurdistan24](https://www.kurdistan24.net/en/story/908985/kurdistan-region-condemns-baghdads-wheat-policy-as-unjust-toward-farmers)). Baghdad agreed to 400,000 t, and up to 85% of Duhok's wheat might go unsold ([Shafaq](https://shafaq.com/en/Kurdistan/85-of-Duhok-wheat-crop-may-remain-unsold)). Iraq's stored water went from about **10 bcm in May 2025**, the lowest in 80 years ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/mena/2025/05/25/iraq-water-reserves-lowest-in-80-years/)), to about **34 bcm in July 2026** ([964media](https://en.964media.com/50317/)). With swings that big, knowing early is the core problem.

Some tools already exist. FAO's WaPOR works with Iraq's water ministry and covers Kurdistan's rain-fed and irrigated land, including the Shemamuk scheme near Erbil ([UN Iraq](https://iraq.un.org/en/277583-fao-wapor-ii-project-continues-develop-capacities-and-increase-technical-follow)). A potato company in Erbil uses Hydrosat for its contract farmers ([PotatoPro](https://www.potatopro.com/node/169866)). A Sulaimani University and KRG ministry study modelled wheat yield from Sentinel-2, but only as research ([Southampton](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/474993)). No free, public, Kurdish-language tool ranks zones by drought risk or estimates the harvest. No farm AI in Kurdish was found anywhere. ChatGPT scores about **70%** on general Sorani questions ([UHD Journal](https://journals.uhd.edu.iq/index.php/uhdjst/article/view/1616)).

## The drought-and-water alarm scores best on fame, proof and ready data

Each use is scored on five tests from the brief: (1) is the problem famous in the Kurdistan Region, (2) is AI proven for it abroad, (3) does the team already have tested data, (4) can it show live AI in a 48-hour demo, and (5) what is the main risk.

| Rank | Use (problem it fixes) | Famous in KRI | Proven abroad | Team data ready | Live AI in demo | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Drought-and-water alarm** (2025 drought, Dukan) | Very high | Strong | Strong, backtested | Medium (calm year, so use replays) | Same engine as the rejected monitor. Accuracy is modest |
| **2** | **Wheat count from space** (Baghdad quota) | Very high | Medium | Good (r = 0.78) | Good (any field in ~3 s) | Political. ±20%. User said no once |
| **3** | **Dam water watch + summer forecast** | Very high | Medium | Good (matches AFP) | Good (any lake in 3–7 s) | Dams are full now. Forecast works for Dukan only |
| 4 | Irrigated-field map for groundwater pressure | Medium | Strong (Spain) | Weak | Medium | No well register. Political |
| 5 | Sorani voice/chat farm advisor | Low as a problem | Strong for SMS/voice. None for chatbots | Medium | Very good | Not a famous problem by itself |
| 6 | Live crop-fire map | Medium | Off-the-shelf | Good | Very good (fires now) | Stubble burning is not crop loss. Small |
| 7 | Satellite drought compensation | Low | Strong, but many failures | Weak | Medium | Cannot tell "sown but failed" from "never sown" |
| 8 | Photo pest and disease AI | Low-medium | Weak in real fields | None | Good | 99% falls to 31%. The team already said no to chemicals |
| 9 | Crop price forecast | High | Weak | None | Medium | Prices follow policy, not data |

The top three all sit on the same water-and-wheat chain, and they share most of the build. The rest drop out for clear reasons. Fires are burning right now: the team counted 170 detections in Erbil in one week, 150 of them on cropland ([team backtest](../evidence/past_seasons/BACKTEST_RESULTS.md)). That makes a good live opening, but stubble burning is not a famous loss. A Sorani voice or chat layer is the *delivery channel* the world evidence says a warning needs. It is not the problem itself. Drought compensation fails on the team's known blind spot: Sentinel-2 cannot tell a field that was sown and failed from one never sown.

## Conclusion

The search for a new problem leads back to the old one. The biggest famous problem (drought and water), the AI use with the strongest world proof (early warning tied to action), and the team's best-tested data all point to the same place. What the rejected monitor lacked was not a better idea. It lacked a named decision. Around the world, impact showed up only where a warning was wired to an action: feed handed out before Kenya's drought peaked, automatic payouts in Kenya's insurance, and India's rule that lets satellite data help trigger drought relief. So the pitch should end with a concrete rule ("when the alarm turns red for most zones by the end of February, this office does this"), not with a map.

In Kurdistan, the weather decides which famous problem shows up. A dry year brings no crop and no water. A wet year brings no buyer. The team's own El Niño call says 2026/27 will most likely be wet, so the headline in spring 2027 is more likely the quota fight than drought. The same satellites and the same model cover both: in a dry year the alarm warns, and in a wet year it counts the harvest. Say this in one closing sentence of the pitch. Do not build it as a second job. Rebuilding four jobs is exactly what the team already rejected.
